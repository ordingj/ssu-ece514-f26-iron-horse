ECE514 Project Preregistration
Using Vineyard Sensor Data to Recommend Irrigation
Team Iron Horse: Joe Ording, Mark Pulido, Ryan Grumich, Spencer Scott        Fall 2026
Hypothesis and decision
We will compare random forest (RF) classification with a simple drying-trend forecast and report logistic regression as an additional benchmark. We hypothesize that RF better predicts a soil matric potential threshold event within 24 hours. Inputs are current soil conditions, recent drying trends, and weather. Success requires test F1 at least 0.05 higher than the drying-trend baseline and recall of at least 0.80.
The decision is whether to recommend irrigate or defer. We fix the dryness threshold at -500 kPa. Both classifiers predict irrigate when their positive-class score is at least 0.70. This cutoff does not imply calibrated 70% risk. Currently dry soil also triggers irrigate, but the primary evaluation includes only currently wetter soil to measure advance warning. This predicts a sensor threshold proxy, not the agronomically optimal irrigation action.
Dataset and prediction unit
We will use the complete export ihv-sensor-data-2026.csv for January 1 through August 31, 2026 PST. Raw rows are individual measurements, not independent examples. We will create one example per matric-sensor location at each clock hour t, combining measurements by device, timestamp, block, and probe. Each location supplies its own target. Results apply to the monitored locations.
Before labeling, we will verify source decoding, kPa units, device-to-block assignments, and available placement records. The CSV field slope denotes terrain position, and label is descriptive metadata. Neither is the outcome. We will decode composite sensor responses, remove documented fault codes and nonfinite values, collapse exact duplicates, and treat conflicting values at the same device, measurement, and timestamp as missing. We will document these rules and source mappings without examining predictive results.
Target and missing observations
Current matric potential is the median of valid readings in (t - 1 hour, t]. The target threshold_crossed_next_24h equals 1 if any valid reading at that location in (t, t + 24 hours] is at or below -500 kPa, and 0 otherwise. Both classes require valid readings in at least 20 of the 24 future hourly bins and no observation gap longer than two hours, including the window boundaries. Other windows remain unlabeled. Future readings are never imputed. Eligible examples require current potential above -500 kPa, at least four valid hourly potential medians in the preceding six hours, and at least 18 in the preceding 24 hours. Invalid current readings or inadequate history produce insufficient data. We will report exclusion counts and forecast coverage so that results cannot conceal missing observations.
Features available at decision time
Features include current matric potential and its least-squares trends over six and 24 hours. We also use each same-block probe's latest soil water content and temperature, retaining separate terrain positions. Weather features are air temperature, humidity, solar radiation, and wind speed averaged over the preceding 24 hours. Rainfall is totaled over that period. Latest probe readings must be no older than one hour. All features use observations at or before t. Device identifiers, location coordinates, descriptive labels, and future measurements are excluded.
Model and baseline
RF uses 300 bootstrap trees, Gini splits, square-root feature sampling, and seed 514. We compare maximum depths {4, 8} and minimum leaf sizes {10, 30}. Ties favor shallower trees, then larger leaves. Logistic regression uses L2 regularization with C = {0.1, 1, 10}, lbfgs, tolerance 0.0001, and 1,000 iterations. Ties favor smaller C. Each model selects the highest validation F1 at the fixed 0.70 cutoff. Other settings use scikit-learn 1.9.1 defaults, without class weighting, class rebalancing, or score calibration. Both models use the same features and eligible examples. Missing predictors receive training medians and separate 0/1 indicators. Entirely missing or constant training features are removed. No other feature selection is permitted. RF inputs remain unscaled. Logistic regression numeric inputs use training means and standard deviations. Indicators remain unscaled.
The primary baseline uses the same current potential and six-hour least-squares trend as RF. Trends are fitted to the valid hourly potential medians, using elapsed hours as the time variable. If the trend is negative and current potential plus 24 times the hourly trend is at or below -500 kPa, the baseline predicts irrigate. Otherwise, it predicts defer. It uses the same eligible examples without tuning.
Chronological split and leakage controls
We will pool the monitored locations while keeping every location on the same calendar split. Training uses January 1-May 31, validation uses June 1-30, and the final test uses July 1-August 31, 2026. We will not randomly split measurement rows. An example is retained only when its complete 24-hour target window ends strictly before the next partition boundary. This removes the final 24 hours of each partition, including August 31 because September outcomes are outside the study period.
Historical inputs may cross an earlier partition boundary because they would already be available at prediction time. After selecting each model's settings on June validation data, we will freeze both pipelines and refit on eligible training and validation examples, including their preprocessing. July-August observations have already been inspected during sensor diagnostics and threshold selection. This retained split therefore supports an exploratory evaluation, not an untouched holdout. We will freeze both pipelines before computing revised-threshold test labels and evaluate each once. Confirmation requires a separately specified, uninspected period.
Metric and success threshold
The primary metric is positive-class F1 pooled across eligible test sensor-hours. A true positive  is an irrigate prediction followed by an observed threshold event. A false positive  is an irrigate prediction without one. A false negative  is a defer prediction followed by one. F1 equals 2TP divided by (2TP + FP + FN), and recall equals TP divided by (TP + FN). An undefined F1 is assigned zero.
We will support the hypothesis only if F1(RF) - F1(drying-trend baseline) is at least 0.05 and RF recall is at least 0.80. Logistic regression is an additional benchmark and does not determine primary success. A 0.05 difference means five absolute F1 percentage points. If either condition fails on an evaluable test set, the amended claim fails. Evaluation requires both classes in development data and at least 20 test sensor-days containing a positive example and 20 containing a negative example. If these requirements are unmet, the claim is not evaluable. We will not change the split or label to make the claim pass.
We will report precision, the confusion matrix, class counts, coverage, and F1 by location for both classifiers and the baselines. To describe uncertainty from correlated hours and overlapping targets, we will report a paired 95% percentile interval for the RF-minus-drying-trend F1 difference using 2,000 seven-day moving-block bootstrap resamples, preserving all locations together, with seed 514. The interval is descriptive and does not alter the success rule.
Registration and interpretation
This preregistration changes our former dryness threshold from -50 to -500 kPa after review of late-season readings. The score cutoff remains 0.70, and the split, coverage, and success thresholds are unchanged. Success would support forecasting the observed sensor threshold under existing management. It would not establish an optimal irrigation trigger or irrigation benefit.
